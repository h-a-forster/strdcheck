# strdcheck report: numerical accuracy of the Python data stack on NIST StRD

Version 0.1.0. Every number below comes from a committed file under [`results/`](results/),
generated by `strdcheck table` from the run described in section 1. Study design:
[docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md) section 6. Accuracy is the log relative error (LRE), the
number of correct significant digits against the NIST certified value, capped at the number
of digits NIST prints (15 for most values, 11 for the nonlinear problems). Each LRE is read
against its *representation ceiling*: the LRE of the exact statistic of the float64-rounded
data (section 2.1). A deficit is ceiling minus LRE.

## Summary

- **Univariate statistics.** Every float64 engine path is within 1.4 digits of the
  representation ceiling on all nine datasets, including NumAcc3/NumAcc4 (ceilings 9.5 and
  8.3). float32 paths keep 0 to 7.6 digits. DuckDB `stddev_samp` on an exact `DECIMAL` column
  returns `DOUBLE`, so it is bounded by the float64 ceiling, not the decimal one.
- **ANOVA.** On SmLs06 (float64 ceiling 9.9), DuckDB group-by on `DOUBLE` and statsmodels
  `anova_lm` keep 6.5 and 6.6 digits, against 9.2-9.3 for numpy, pandas and polars group-by
  aggregates and 10.2 for `scipy.stats.f_oneway`. On SmLs09 both keep 0 digits (ceiling 3.9;
  numpy 3.3).
- **Linear least squares.** Results follow conditioning (Filip cond(X) = 1.8e15, Pontius
  1.4e13, Longley 4.9e9). On Filip the default singular-value cutoffs of `numpy.linalg.lstsq`,
  `torch.linalg.lstsq` and statsmodels `OLS(method="pinv")` drop one singular value and return
  0 correct digits; `numpy.polynomial.Polynomial.fit` (scaled domain) gets 13.8. On Pontius the
  SVD-based paths keep 6.2 digits and the QR-based paths 12.1-13.2.
- **Nonlinear least squares from random starts** (27 problems x 20 starts, spread sigma = 1):
  about a third of fits report success but match the certified parameters to fewer than
  4 digits. Silent-failure rate 0.31 (95% CI 0.18-0.45) for `least_squares(method="trf")`
  with an analytic Jacobian and tight tolerances, 0.32 (0.19-0.46) for `curve_fit` defaults,
  0.41 (0.26-0.57) for trf with a 2-point Jacobian and default tolerances. Most silent fits
  (0.22-0.30 of all fits) also have a residual sum of squares that is wrong to 4 digits, so
  they are not equivalent minima.
- **Pre-specified comparisons.** Only C4 (tight vs default tolerances, trf, analytic Jacobian)
  is significant after Holm correction: silent rate 0.311 vs 0.378, difference -0.067
  (95% CI -0.146 to -0.019), Holm p = 0.005; no problem got worse with tight tolerances.
- **Large n (tiled data).** At n up to 1e6, numpy and pandas keep all 15 digits of the SD of
  tiled NumAcc3/NumAcc4/PiDigits; polars (eager and streaming), DuckDB and torch keep 8.9-11.2
  digits in their worst cell. DuckDB with 3 threads and polars streaming were not bitwise
  repeatable across three in-process runs in 6 and 8 of 18 cells.
- No result here is a claimed bug. Every notable cell is explained by documented behaviour
  (cutoffs, defaults, accumulation order, algorithm choice) or by conditioning; standalone
  reproductions are in [`repros/`](repros/).

## 2. Validation

### 2.1 References

- **V1, certified values.** Exact rational arithmetic on the decimal data reproduces the
  236 certified values of the univariate, ANOVA and linear suites, and mpmath Gauss-Newton
  refinement (40 digits) reproduces the 294 nonlinear ones, all to within 0.67 digits of the
  printed precision ([`results/certified_check.csv`](results/certified_check.csv)). For the
  nonlinear problems the reference is a refinement of the certified point, not an
  independent global solve.
- **Ceilings.** For each quantity the exact statistic is computed again from the
  float64-rounded and float32-rounded data ([`results/ceilings.csv`](results/ceilings.csv)).
  An engine can beat its ceiling by a fraction of a digit by chance (torch.f64 on Michelso:
  13.9 against 13.8).
- **Controls.** The exact control reaches the cap everywhere. The negative controls lose what
  the textbooks predict: one-pass SD on NumAcc3 keeps 2.2 digits against a ceiling of 9.5 and
  fails on NumAcc4; normal equations keep 0 digits on Filip and 6.2 on Wampler1.
- **Tiled references.** For k copies of a dataset the exact mean and SD are derived from the
  exact sums of the original data; k = 1 reproduces the float64 reference exactly (test M8).
- **Repeatability.** All Part A units gave bitwise-identical results across three repeats.

### 2.2 Tests

`uv run pytest`: 220 passed, 2 skipped; with `--runslow`: 222 passed. The suite checks exact reference values, the parser on all
58 files, every model Jacobian against mpmath, adapters on synthetic data with known answers,
the checkpoint store under interruption, the statistics (Wilson, bootstrap, the sign-flip test
against its exact value, Holm) and a small end-to-end study.

### 3.4 Notable cells and triage (DESIGN 6.2, 6.6)

[`results/notable.csv`](results/notable.csv) lists 141 (dataset, adapter) cells: 85 with a
float64 deficit above 2 digits where the ceiling is at least 6 (4 ANOVA, 81 linear) and 56
nonlinear fits from the NIST starts that report success with fewer than 4 correct digits.
The `best_engine_lre` column splits the 85 deficit cells into two groups.

- **Conditioning (49 cells)**: within 2 digits of the best float64 engine on the same dataset
  (Wampler1/3/5, Longley, Norris and others). Every algorithm loses these digits.
- **Algorithm or default (36 cells)**, more than 2 digits below the best engine:
  - *Filip, rank cutoff* (numpy and torch `lstsq`, statsmodels pinv): the default `rcond`
    treats the smallest singular value as zero; documented behaviour, recovered with `rcond`
    near 0 ([`repros/filip_rcond.py`](repros/filip_rcond.py)). scipy gelsd/gelss keep 5.7
    digits of the coefficients but 2.8-3.0 of the derived standard errors; numpy.polyfit keeps
    7.5 digits of the coefficients, but its covariance gives 0 digits for the standard errors.
  - *Filip, statsmodels `OLS(method="qr")`*: coefficients good to 8.1 digits, but some
    diagonal entries of the parameter covariance are negative and `bse` is nan
    ([`repros/statsmodels_qr_filip.py`](repros/statsmodels_qr_filip.py)).
  - *Pontius and Wampler2, SVD-based solvers* (gelsd, gelss, pinv): 4-7 digits below the
    QR-based solvers on the same data. Columns of very different scale (x and x squared up to
    1e13) suit QR better than an unscaled SVD; numpy.polyfit, which scales columns, does not
    lose them.
  - *Wampler1/4/5*: several solvers 2.0-2.5 digits below `Polynomial.fit`, which maps x to
    [-1, 1] before fitting.
  - *Longley, statsmodels qr*: standard error of b6 at 8.0 digits against 14.8 for the best
    path.
  - *SmLs03 and SmLs06, DuckDB `DOUBLE` group-by and statsmodels `anova_lm`*: section 3.2.
  - *Norris, `scipy.stats.linregress`*: standard error of the slope at 11.8 against 14.2.

None of these is reported as a defect. Each follows from a documented default or the choice
of algorithm, and a user can avoid it (a lower `rcond`, QR, centring, `Polynomial.fit`).

## 4. Part B: nonlinear least squares from random starts

Starts are `b0 = b* exp(sigma z)`, `z ~ N(0, I)`, seeded per (problem, start, sigma), with
sigma = 1.0 as the primary spread. A fit is *silent* when the solver reports success and the
minimum parameter LRE is below tau = 4, and *flagged* when the solver reports failure. Rates
are computed per problem and averaged over the 27 problems; intervals are 95%
cluster-bootstrap intervals over problems (10,000 resamples).

### 4.2 Silent fits at an equivalent minimum (secondary)

Some models have exchangeable components (the terms of Lanczos, the two peaks of Gauss1-3,
the cycles of ENSO) or parameters the data barely determine, so a fit can match the certified
RSS while its parameters differ from the certified ones. Splitting silent fits by whether the
RSS agrees with the certified minimum to 4 digits
([`results/nonlinear_silent_split.csv`](results/nonlinear_silent_split.csv)): in every
configuration 0.04-0.12 of fits are silent at an equivalent minimum and 0.22-0.30 are silent
with a wrong RSS (trf analytic tight: 0.050 and 0.261; curve_fit defaults: 0.072 and 0.244).
Most silent failures are therefore wrong answers, not relabellings. This split was added after
the primary analysis was specified and is secondary.

## 6. Limitations

- **One platform.** One CPU, one OS and one BLAS/LAPACK build (scipy-openblas 0.3.34). LAPACK
  paths (lstsq drivers, QR, SVD) can give different digits on other builds or CPUs; the
  univariate and group-by results depend less on the platform.
- **Size.** The primary analysis uses the quick configuration: 20 starts per problem at one
  spread. The full configuration (`configs/full.toml`: 200 starts, three spreads, Part C up to
  n = 2e7) is documented but was not run. At the measured Part B throughput of about 2.3-2.7
  units per second on 3 workers it takes 2-2.5 hours and should be split (one spread per run)
  to keep each run under 45 minutes.
- **Random-start design.** Multiplicative log-normal perturbations keep parameter signs and
  scale with the size of each certified parameter; other designs (additive, box-uniform)
  would give other rates. Rates are conditional on this design and on the 27 NIST models.
- **Outcome definition.** "Silent" compares parameters with the certified point; section 4.2
  shows that a minority of those fits reach an equivalent minimum. The tau = 2/4/6 rows show
  how much the rates depend on the threshold.
- **Derived quantities.** Standard errors, residual SD and R squared for engines that do not
  report them are derived by strdcheck from the engine's solution with one fixed QR/SVD
  procedure; their LREs mix engine and derivation error and are marked `derived` in the
  tables.
- **Nonlinear references** are mpmath refinements of the certified values, checked against
  them, not independent global solves.
- **Engines and versions.** Results hold for the versions in section 1. GPU paths, R, SAS and
  Stata are out of scope; for the latter see McCullough (1998, 1999) and Hill, Du, Johnson
  and McCullough (2024).
- **Not bug reports.** Notable cells were triaged against documentation and with standalone
  reproductions; none is claimed as a defect or has been filed upstream.
